Movie Webpage Project (Attack on Titan: The Cycle Reborn)

Context

For the Movie Webpage Project, my partner Elisa and I were challenged to create a fictional movie website using HTML and CSS. The purpose of the project was to apply everything we had learned about webpage structure and styling while working collaboratively with a partner. In addition to meeting the HTML and CSS requirements, we also needed to create a plan, divide responsibilities, use local images, and design a webpage that looked visually appealing and organized.

Since both of us were familiar with Attack on Titan, we decided to create our own original sequel called Attack on Titan: The Cycle Reborn. Rather than retelling the original story, we imagined a future that takes place thousands of years after the anime ends. Our story explores the possibility that Eren's influence never completely disappeared and that the source of Titan power survived deep beneath the giant tree shown at the end of the series.

Elisa and I divided the work efficiently by assigning specific sections to each partner. I worked on the movie title, story introduction, Eren's Return section, New Titans section, Google Fonts, span highlights, and newsletter form. Elisa worked on The Discovery of the Tree section, The New Host section, The Final Choice section, the color scheme, styling, local images, and submit button. Together we reviewed the webpage, fixed bugs, adjusted the layout, and ensured that every project requirement was completed.

Content

Our movie webpage tells the story of a descendant of Historia who discovers the giant tree that grew from Eren Yeager's grave. Throughout the webpage, visitors learn how the Hallucigenia survived, how Eren's consciousness continued through The Paths, and how entirely new Titan mutations begin appearing throughout the world.

To meet the project requirements, we included headings, paragraphs, divs, spans, Google Fonts, local images, inputs, submit buttons, and CSS styling. We also completed the extra credit portion by creating a sequel page called Attack on Titan: Freedom's End and connecting both webpages together through navigation links.

Some HTML concepts we applied included headings, images, links, forms, division containers, and spans. For example, we used div sections to organize the story: 


<div class="story-section">
    <h2>Eren's Return</h2>
    <p>Story content...</p>
</div>

We also used span tags to highlight important story elements such as Eren Yeager, Freedom, Hallucigenia, and The Paths: 


<span class="highlight">Eren Yeager</span>

For CSS, we practiced selectors, the box model, colors, Google Fonts, alignment, hover effects, and visual styling. One example was styling the movie title with an ID selector: 


#main-title {
    color: #8B0000;
    border: 4px solid #8B0000;
    padding: 20px;
}

Reflection

Challenges

One of our biggest challenges was creating a storyline that felt original while still remaining connected to the Attack on Titan universe. We wanted the movie to feel believable instead of simply repeating the original plot. After discussing different ideas, we focused on the giant tree shown at the end of Attack on Titan and used it as the foundation for our sequel. This decision helped us build an entirely new story while still respecting existing lore.

Another challenge involved styling and organization. During development, some sections looked inconsistent because spacing, image sizes, and alignment were different throughout the webpage. To fix this, we adjusted our CSS properties such as margin, padding, border, width, and text alignment until the webpage looked more consistent and professional.

We also spent time experimenting with hover effects, gradients, fonts, borders, and colors. While these changes were not required, they improved the overall appearance of the webpage and made the movie feel more immersive.

Takeaways

One of my biggest takeaways from this project is the importance of planning. Because Elisa and I created a detailed plan before coding, we were able to divide responsibilities efficiently and avoid confusion later in development.

I also learned that HTML and CSS work best when combined. HTML provides the structure of a webpage, while CSS transforms that structure into something visually appealing. Through this project, I gained a better understanding of selectors, the box model, forms, fonts, images, spans, divs, and webpage organization.

Another takeaway was the importance of collaboration. Since both partners were responsible for different parts of the project, communication was necessary to ensure that our sections matched each other stylistically and narratively.

Next Steps

If I continued developing this project, I would expand the storyline even further by adding character biographies, detailed Titan profiles, additional movie scenes, and more interactive features using JavaScript. I would also create custom artwork and animations to make the webpage feel even more like an official movie website.

I would also like to continue building sequel pages and further develop the world we created. Since the extra credit sequel introduced a completely new ending to the story, there are many opportunities to continue expanding the Attack on Titan universe we created through this project.
